\begin{abstract}
Modern monocular depth estimators are strong in single-view prediction \cite{eigen2014depth,ranftl2022midas,yang2024depthanything,yang2024depthanythingv2}, but geometric consistency across nearby views remains a practical bottleneck in RGB-D pipelines. We present \textbf{EagleVision}, a lightweight adaptation framework that can wrap \emph{any} pretrained depth estimator with a compact residual adapter and optimize it using an explicit source$\rightarrow$target$\rightarrow$source geometric cycle. The method is parameter-efficient by construction: the large pretrained backbone is kept frozen, while only a small residual head is trained. For controlled experiments, this paper instantiates EagleVision with a pretrained Depth Anything V2-Small metric model \cite{yang2024depthanythingv2,dai2017scannet}. Beyond improving geometric consistency, our optimization also improves depth-estimation-specific quantitative metrics (AbsRel, RMSE, Depth L1, and $\delta_1$), showing that geometry-driven adaptation can directly benefit depth accuracy. Project page and code: \href{https://github.com/alooboii/EagleVision}{\texttt{[EagleVision]}}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Monocular depth estimation has advanced rapidly through large-scale pretraining, transformer backbones, and robust dense-prediction heads \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit,oquab2023dinov2,ranftl2021dpt,ranftl2022midas,yang2024depthanything,yang2024depthanythingv2}. At the same time, many deployed systems consume depth inside \emph{multi-view} geometric pipelines rather than in isolated single-image evaluation. In indoor RGB-D settings, predicted depth is repeatedly used by reprojection, visibility reasoning, and cross-view consistency checks. This creates a practical gap: a model can be strong in frame-wise depth quality yet still underperform when the same depth must remain coherent under view transformation.

Existing directions include full-model fine-tuning, self-supervised geometric consistency, and multi-term reconstruction objectives \cite{godard2019monodepth2,ranftl2022midas,bhat2023zoedepth}. While effective, these strategies can be expensive and can overwrite useful pretrained priors. EagleVision addresses this by separating \emph{capacity} from \emph{adaptation}: retain a strong pretrained estimator, freeze it, and learn only a small residual correction under a geometry-aware objective.

The key method is backbone-agnostic. EagleVision treats the pretrained estimator as a pluggable module and optimizes a lightweight adapter around it. This means the approach is not tied to a single depth family in principle; for controlled empirical evidence, the main adaptation experiments in this paper instantiate the method on a pretrained Depth Anything V2-Small metric backbone \cite{yang2024depthanythingv2}.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A backbone-agnostic geometric adaptation framework that wraps pretrained monocular depth estimators with a lightweight residual adapter while preserving frozen backbone priors.
  \item A geometry-grounded round-trip training objective that couples forward and backward warping consistency.
  \item A reproducible evaluation protocol showing that the proposed optimization improves geometric consistency and also improves depth-estimation-specific quantitative metrics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Overview}
EagleVision has three functional components: (1) a frozen pretrained depth backbone, (2) a small trainable residual adapter, and (3) an explicit geometric warping module. The backbone supplies base depth, the adapter predicts a correction, and the round-trip warp loop provides cross-view geometric supervision. \paragraph{Terminology and Scope.}
We use \emph{backbone} to mean a pretrained monocular depth estimator, \emph{adapter} to mean the lightweight trainable residual head, and \emph{warp} to mean geometry-based projection between camera views using depth and pose under standard multi-view projective geometry \cite{hartley2003multiple}. A \emph{round-trip cycle} denotes source$\rightarrow$target$\rightarrow$source warping. The methodological contribution is the optimization strategy around a frozen pretrained backbone, not a new backbone architecture.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Each training sample contains a source tuple $(I_s, D_s^{gt}, K_s, T_s)$ and a target tuple $(I_t, D_t^{gt}, K_t, T_t)$. Here $I$ is RGB, $D^{gt}$ is metric depth, $K$ is intrinsics, and $T$ is pose in the standard pinhole-camera formulation \cite{hartley2003multiple}. Relative transforms are
\begin{equation}
T_{s\rightarrow t}=T_tT_s^{-1}, \qquad T_{t\rightarrow s}=T_sT_t^{-1}.
\end{equation}
For homogeneous source pixel $\tilde{u}$, source 3D point is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_s(u)=D_s^{gt}(u)K_s^{-1}\tilde{u}.
\end{equation}
It is transformed and projected to target image space:
\begin{align}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{p}_t(u)\\
1
\end{bmatrix}
&=T_{s\rightarrow t}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{p}_s(u)\\
1
\end{bmatrix}, \\
\tilde{u}_t &\sim K_t\mathbf{p}_t(u).
\end{align}
After visibility filtering with z-buffering \cite{catmull1974subdivision}, forward warp returns
\begin{equation}
I_{s\rightarrow t}^{warp}, \quad D_{s\rightarrow t}^{warp}, \quad M_t.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Residual Depth Adaptation}
Let $f_{\theta_0}$ be a frozen pretrained depth estimator and $g_{\phi}$ be the trainable adapter. The first pass predicts
\begin{equation}
D_t^{base}=f_{\theta_0}(I_{s\rightarrow t}^{warp}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_t=g_{\phi}\!\left([I_{s\rightarrow t}^{warp},D_t^{base}]\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D_t^{adapt}=\max(D_t^{base}+R_t,\epsilon).
\end{equation}
This residual form preserves pretrained priors while allowing targeted domain correction.

\subsection{Depth Fusion and Round-Trip Prediction}
Forward splatting leaves invalid target pixels. We fuse warped depth and adapted depth as
\begin{equation}
D_t^{fused}=M_t\odot D_{s\rightarrow t}^{warp} + (1-M_t)\odot D_t^{adapt}.
\end{equation}
Backward warp with $T_{t\rightarrow s}$ produces
\begin{equation}
I_{t\rightarrow s}^{recon}, \quad D_s^{warp}, \quad M_s.
\end{equation}
Then the \emph{same shared depth estimator} is invoked again:
\begin{equation}
D_s^{pred}=f_{\theta_0,\phi}(I_{t\rightarrow s}^{recon}).
\end{equation}
Importantly, $D_s^{pred}$ is not a direct backward-warp output; it is the second estimator pass on reconstructed source-view input.

\subsection{Training Objective}
The final objective combines target-view and cycle-view supervision:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{total}=
\lambda_{trgb}\mathcal{L}_{trgb}+
\lambda_{crgb}\mathcal{L}_{crgb}+
\lambda_{cdep}\mathcal{L}_{cdep}+
\lambda_{tdep}\mathcal{L}_{tdep},
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{trgb}
&=\frac{1}{|M_t|}\sum_{u\in M_t}\left|I_{s\rightarrow t}^{warp}(u)-I_t(u)\right|, \\
\mathcal{L}_{crgb}
&=\frac{1}{|M_s|}\sum_{u\in M_s}\left|I_{t\rightarrow s}^{recon}(u)-I_s(u)\right|, \\
\mathcal{L}_{cdep}
&=\frac{1}{|M_s|}\sum_{u\in M_s}\left|D_s^{pred}(u)-D_s^{gt}(u)\right|, \\
\mathcal{L}_{tdep}
&=\frac{1}{|\Omega_t^{valid}|}\sum_{u\in\Omega_t^{valid}}\left|D_t^{adapt}(u)-D_t^{gt}(u)\right|.
\end{align}

\subsection{Quantitative Metrics}
Because the contribution targets depth improvement under geometric supervision, primary reporting focuses on depth-specific and geometry-consistency metrics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AbsRel} ($\downarrow$): $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \frac{|d_i-\hat{d}_i|}{d_i}$ \cite{eigen2014depth}.
  \item \textbf{RMSE} ($\downarrow$): $\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i(d_i-\hat{d}_i)^2}$ \cite{eigen2014depth}.
  \item \textbf{Depth L1} ($\downarrow$): mean absolute metric-depth error.
  \item \textbf{$\delta_1$} ($\uparrow$): fraction where $\max(\hat{d}_i/d_i, d_i/\hat{d}_i) < 1.25$ \cite{eigen2014depth}.
  \item \textbf{Reprojection Depth L1} ($\downarrow$): depth consistency after geometric round-trip.
\end{itemize}
We also report PSNR/SSIM reconstruction indicators for geometric diagnostics (SSIM \cite{wang2004ssim}), but depth metrics are the primary success criteria for this phase.

\section{Discussion}
The central empirical finding is that a geometric adaptation objective, applied to a frozen pretrained estimator through a tiny residual head, can improve depth quality in a measurable way. On the ScanNet paired protocol, EagleVision reduces AbsRel from 0.461546 to 0.344948 (\textbf{25.26\%} relative reduction), RMSE from 2.371236 to 2.187455 (\textbf{7.75\%} reduction), and reprojection depth L1 from 2.208435 to 2.020791 (\textbf{8.50\%} reduction). Threshold accuracy also improves, with $\delta_1$ rising from 0.312454 to 0.395220 (relative +\textbf{26.49\%}).

These results matter because EagleVision is not a heavyweight replacement model; it is an optimization layer around a large frozen backbone. The positive depth-metric gains show that the proposed geometric objective does not only help consistency signals, but also directly improves depth-estimation-specific quantitative performance.

\section{Limitations}
Although EagleVision improves depth predictions over the frozen pretrained baseline, the present work has clear scope limits.

First, this study focuses on depth-map adaptation as a \emph{prior} stage and does not evaluate a full downstream novel-view-synthesis or reconstruction system. Accordingly, we do not claim downstream-task improvements in this paper.

Second, the current evidence is based on relatively limited ScanNet-domain training/validation coverage. Results should be interpreted as controlled validation evidence rather than full-domain generalization across all indoor settings.

Third, while EagleVision is backbone-agnostic in formulation, adaptation training here is only instantiated on a pretrained DA2-Small backbone. We do not yet train the same objective across multiple pretrained backbone families under matched protocols.

Fourth, we also do not yet report multi-dataset adaptation training over one fixed pretrained backbone. Cross-dataset robustness of the adapted prior therefore remains an open question.

Fifth, we do not yet evaluate whether adapted depth gains transfer to concrete downstream tasks. This is a key next validation step for the framework.

\section{Future Improvements}
Several next steps can substantially strengthen the method while preserving its parameter-efficient core.

(1) \textbf{Depth-focused supervision}: extend the objective with additional depth-oriented terms such as log-depth, scale-invariant log-depth, depth-gradient, and normal-consistency losses, while keeping RGB-cycle terms auxiliary.

(2) \textbf{Better loss balancing}: further study the weighting between RGB reconstruction and depth-specific objectives, including staged schedules that prioritize depth quality early and consistency terms later.

(3) \textbf{Broader adaptation evidence}: train across multiple datasets on the same pretrained backbone to test domain robustness of the learned residual prior.

(4) \textbf{Multi-backbone adaptation}: run matched adaptation training on multiple pretrained estimator families (for example DA2, DPT, MiDaS-style setups) to rigorously validate backbone-agnostic behavior.

(5) \textbf{Downstream validation}: measure transfer to tasks that consume depth priors, including view-synthesis and geometry-heavy pipelines.

\section{Conclusion}
EagleVision provides a practical geometric adaptation strategy for pretrained monocular depth estimators: keep the large backbone frozen, train a small residual adapter, and optimize with explicit round-trip consistency. In the reported DA2-Small instantiation, this optimization improves both geometry consistency and core depth-estimation metrics while updating only a tiny fraction of parameters. The next stage is clear: broaden backbone and dataset coverage and validate transfer to downstream tasks.
